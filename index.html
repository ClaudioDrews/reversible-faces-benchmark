03

The geometry

The illusion is not magic: it falls out of a half-turn, a silhouette envelope and compromise strokes. Every tab below is a hands-on lab.

R is an involution

R(p) = 2C − p  →  (x, y) ↦ (1000 − x, 1000 − y)

On the 1000 × 1000 page, with centre C = (500, 500), the half-turn sends each point to the diametrically opposite one. Applying it twice returns the original point: R∘R = id. That is what makes it an involution, and it is why the geometry is so cheap to check — the model can verify coordinates without ever seeing the drawing.

R is a proper rotation (determinant +1), not a mirror. Mirroring (scale(-1, 1)) has determinant −1 and produces a different genre of illusion. A half-turn flips vertical order: what sits above A's eyes ends up below B's eyes. Straight lines keep their direction: a cup ∪ becomes a dome ∩.

One stroke, two readings

When the eyes are shared — Whistler's anchor — a stroke that is a feature of A at height yA (on the page) is a feature of B at height −yB in B's own upright frame. In page coordinates: yA = 1000 − yB.

Nothing below A's eyes can stay below B's eyes. A's mouth (y = 680) has to become B's hat brim (y = 350): 330 px apart, far beyond the 30 px tolerance.

The canonical ladder

The prompt kit describes a canonical face-height ladder (H = 600 px), with the eye line at normalized y = 0 and page position y = 500 + H·y. These are construction heuristics, not laws of perception or measurements of Whistler’s drawings.

A design tolerance, not a measured perceptual threshold. The prompt uses ε ≈ 0.05H (30 px when H = 600 px) to guide placement. It has not been calibrated with human observers. Hair, hats, beards, collars and wrinkles are useful degrees of freedom—not guarantees of recognition.

Compromise strokes

Imagine a shared stroke with target point a in A’s page coordinates and target point b in B’s own upright frame. B’s page target is R(b). A single point can fall within ε of both targets exactly when ‖a − R(b)‖ ≤ 2ε. The diagram illustrates this point-wise geometric criterion; recognizable strokes require additional perceptual judgment.

s* = (wA·a + wB·R(b)) / (wA + wB)  →  com pesos iguais: s* = (a + R(b)) / 2

Drag the target points to see the two tolerance disks. The midpoint minimizes equal-weight squared distance, but a mathematically feasible compromise does not necessarily look like a face.

04

Symmetry decomposition

The half-turn splits any rasterized image into an even part and an odd part: I₊ = ½(I + R·I), I₋ = ½(I − R·I). Inspect both components and compare a rotational-asymmetry diagnostic against the reference. This is algebra, not a face recognizer.

ρ measures pixelwise difference under a 180° turn. A very low value warns of near-rotational symmetry, which is not enough for two distinct readings. Larger values do not imply better faces; ρ is never the benchmark’s pass/fail criterion.

07

Method, limits and an honest reading

The benchmark is exploratory. This section exists so nobody reads the numbers above as a leaderboard.

Not a leaderboard

One human evaluator, who is also the benchmark's author. One evaluated output per model per prompt. No validated scoring scale and no statistical inference. The results are observations that motivate controlled experiments, not measures of capability.

Capability ≠ reliability ≠ efficiency

A verdict on one output answers only one of three questions. Capability appears when the model delivers. Reliability (how often?) is unmeasured: one run per model. Efficiency (tokens, calls, renders, dollars) is recorded, but the conditions differ between rounds.

The token-budget rule

A model that exhausts its token budget verifying its own geometry is a task-completion failure under that inference regime, not evidence of geometric incapacity. The same model delivered an identifiable result once reasoning was disabled.

The pilot and round 4

The pilot (round 0) was not human-evaluated. Round 4 used tools, but several things changed at once: the effect of the tools is not isolated. MiniMax was run twice and only one run entered the evaluated set — a selection between two runs, described as such.

The caveats that travel with any number

    The SVG file contract

    • Standalone SVG, xmlns, viewBox="0 0 1000 1000", width/height = 1000, valid XML, at most 300 KB.
    • Forbidden: text of any kind, <image>, <foreignObject>, <script>, gradients, blur/filters, drop shadows and any directional lighting.
    • Allowed: line work, hatching, flat fills, a small flat palette, white space.
    • From round 3 on, <title>, <desc> and <metadata> are removed from the delivered SVG.
