# A worked example: how one output achieves the double reading

This walks through one delivered SVG from round 3 (`rounds/round-03/svg/minimax-m3.1-flash_prompt-v5.svg`, generated by MiniMax with prompt v5) to show what "one set of strokes, two faces" means in practice. It is an illustration of the mechanism, not a claim that this is a good drawing.

![Round 3 MiniMax output, upright and rotated](../assets/previews/round-03__minimax-m3.1-flash_prompt-v5.png)

A point (x, y) of the upright face lands, after the half turn, at (1000 − x, 1000 − y). Only the **vertical** extent is given below, because it decides which feature a stroke can be in the other reading (the mirror in x is handled by the same formula). Numbers are approximate extents read from the path coordinates (control points included).

| Stroke as drawn (face A) | Page y in A | Page y after rotation (face B) | Role in A | Role in B |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Solid black block with a flared base and a small black finial on top | 52 – 208 (hat crown, finial) and 850 – 946 (base) | 792 – 948 and 54 – 150 | A's hat (a small crown over a wide brim) | B's **chin and goatee** (crown/finial) and B's wide-brimmed **hat top** (the flared base) |
| Large solid block under the face | 706 – 872 | 128 – 294 | A's **beard / jaw mass** | B's **hat dome** |
| Dark moustache-shaped mass | 600 – 668 | 332 – 400 | A's **moustache** | B's heavy dark **fringe or brow band** |
| Red lens-shaped stroke | 666 – 694 | 306 – 334 | A's **smile** (a ∪) | B's **red hat band** (a ∩) |
| Small outlined shape between the cheeks | 545 – 598 | 402 – 455 | A's **nose** | B's small **forehead mark** between the brows |
| Small dark teardrop on the forehead | 344 – 404 | 596 – 656 | A's **forehead mark** | B's **nose** |
| Thin curved stroke on the forehead | 300 – 344 | 656 – 700 | A's **hairline curl** | B's **mouth** |
| Two arched strokes above the eyes | 428 – 458 | 542 – 572 | A's **raised brows** | B's **eye-bags** (arches become cups) |
| Scalloped black band under the hat | 202 – 310 | 690 – 798 | A's **hat fringe** | B's **scalloped moustache / beard line** |
| Eyes (two circles at (415, 500), (585, 500)) | 473 – 527 | 473 – 527 | **shared** | **shared** |

Strokes drawn only for B sit in a group `<g transform="rotate(180 500 500)">`: the two slanted lines (B's angry brows), the two small arcs (B's eyelids) and the small red clasp. In A they land where they are inert: the slanted brows turn into two thin cheek lines, and the clasp becomes a red badge on A's hat. This is the "B-only strokes must be few, light and hidden in A" rule of the prompt.

What this shows about the task: nearly every visible stroke has to be read twice, and the placement of each is dictated by the dual-function dictionary (hat dome ↔ mouth, beard ↔ hat, brows ↔ eye-bags). A model that adds decoration before these pairs are fixed tends to break one of the readings, which is what happened in round 2.
